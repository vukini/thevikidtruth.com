# French: Vocabulary

## The core idea in plain words

Every French noun has a **gender** — masculine or feminine — and you have to learn it together with the word. Not *maison*, but **la maison**. Not *livre*, but **le livre**. The gender changes the article, the adjectives and sometimes the pronoun, so a word learned without its article is only half learned.

This page has two parts: a themed starter list for **the home and daily life**, and the words I collected while reading (my old *Vocab List 1* cards), each with its gender, meaning and an example sentence. The article is attached to every noun. Learn them in the little phrase, out loud.

## An analogy: name tags at a party

Think of every noun arriving at the party wearing a coloured name tag: blue for masculine (*le / un*), red for feminine (*la / une*). The tag doesn't mean anything about the thing itself — a table isn't feminine in any real sense — it just tells everyone else (articles, adjectives) how to talk to it. Learn the face *and* the tag at the same time, or you'll be guessing all evening.

## The article cheat sheet

| | Masculine | Feminine | Before a vowel or mute h | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the | **le** | **la** | **l'** | **les** |
| a / an | **un** | **une** | (un / une) | **des** |
| some | **du** | **de la** | **de l'** | **des** |

For nouns starting with a vowel I give the indefinite article (*un / une*) too, because *l'* hides the gender.

## Gender clues (rules of thumb, not laws)

- Usually **feminine**: endings *-tion, -sion* (la nation), *-té* (la liberté), *-ette* (la fourchette), *-ure* (la voiture), *-ence / -ance* (la chance).
- Usually **masculine**: endings *-ment* (le bâtiment), *-age* (le fromage — but **la** page, **la** plage, **l'**image f), *-eau* (le couteau — but **l'**eau f, **la** peau), *-isme*, and most words ending in a consonant.
- Days, months, seasons, languages and metric units are masculine: *le lundi, le français, le mètre*.

## A worked example: one noun, four changes

Take **la fenêtre** (window), feminine:

- *La fenêtre est **ouverte**.* — The window is open. (adjective takes *-e*)
- *Ferme-**la**, s'il te plaît.* — Close it, please. (pronoun *la*)
- *Une **grande** fenêtre.* — A big window. (*grand* → *grande*)
- *Elle est cassée.* — It's broken. (*elle*, even though it's a thing)

Now swap to **le mur** (wall), masculine: *Le mur est blanc. Repeins-le. Un grand mur. Il est sale.* Every word that touches the noun changes. That's why the article must come attached.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Gender follows meaning."** *La personne* is feminine even for a man; *le bébé* is masculine even for a girl.
- **"Words ending in -e are feminine."** Often, but *le verre, le beurre, le musée, le lycée* are masculine.
- **"I'll learn the genders later."** You won't. Learn *la clé*, never *clé*.
- **"Same spelling, same word."** *Le livre* = the book; *la livre* = the pound. *Le tour* = the tour; *la tour* = the tower.
