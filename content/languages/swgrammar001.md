# Swahili: Basic Grammar

This page was an empty placeholder. Here is the core grammar the rest of the Swahili pages use, built around the root *-soma* (read, study).

## 1. Pronouns and subject markers

Every verb starts with a subject marker. The pronoun itself is optional: *ninasoma* already means "I am reading".

| Pronoun | English | Subject marker | Negative marker | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mimi | I | ni- | si- | **ni**nasoma, I read |
| wewe | you | u- | hu- | **u**nasoma, you read |
| yeye | he / she | a- | ha- | **a**nasoma, she reads |
| sisi | we | tu- | hatu- | **tu**nasoma, we read |
| nyinyi | you all | m- | ham- | **m**nasoma, you all read |
| wao | they | wa- | hawa- | **wa**nasoma, they read |

## 2. Tense markers

Subject + **tense** + root:

| Marker | Tense | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| -na- | present | ninasoma | I am reading / I read |
| -li- | past | nilisoma | I read (yesterday) |
| -ta- | future | nitasoma | I will read |
| -me- | perfect | nimesoma | I have read |

*Nimefurahi* (I'm glad) is the perfect of *-furahi*: literally "I have become happy".

## 3. Negatives

Use the negative marker. The present negative drops *-na-* and changes the final *-a* to *-i*; the past uses *-ku-*; the future keeps *-ta-*.

| Positive | Negative | English |
|---|---|---|
| ninasoma | sisomi | I (don't) read |
| anasoma | hasomi | she (doesn't) read |
| nilisoma | sikusoma | I read / didn't read |
| nitasoma | sitasoma | I will / won't read |
| ni (is) | si (is not) | *Mimi si Mtanzania.* I'm not Tanzanian. |

## 4. "To be" and "to have"

- **ni** = is/am/are, **si** = is not: *Jane ni mwanafunzi.* Jane is a student.
- **-ko / -po / -mo** = is located: *Benki iko wapi?* Where is the bank?
- **-na** = have: *nina* I have, *una* you have, *ana* he/she has, *tuna*, *mna*, *wana*. *Nina kiu.* I'm thirsty ("I have thirst").

## 5. Noun classes

Each class has a singular and plural prefix, and the agreement spreads to adjectives, possessives, demonstratives and verbs.

| Class | Singular | Plural | Typical meaning | "my" | "this" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| m-/wa- | mtoto | watoto | people | wangu / wangu | huyu / hawa |
| m-/mi- | mti | miti | trees, plants | wangu / yangu | huu / hii |
| ji-/ma- | jina | majina | assorted, pairs | langu / yangu | hili / haya |
| ki-/vi- | kitabu | vitabu | things, languages | changu / vyangu | hiki / hivi |
| n- | nyumba | nyumba | many loanwords, animals | yangu / zangu | hii / hizi |
| u- | ufunguo | funguo | abstract, long things | wangu / zangu | huu / hizi |

Examples:

- *Mtoto mzuri anasoma.* The good child is reading.
- *Watoto wazuri wanasoma.* The good children are reading.
- *Kitabu changu kiko hapa.* My book is here.
- *Vitabu vyangu viko hapa.* My books are here.

## 6. Questions

Intonation alone makes a question: *Unasoma Kiswahili?* Do you study Swahili? Or put *je* in front: *Je, unasoma Kiswahili?* Question words usually come at the end: *Unaishi wapi?* Where do you live?
