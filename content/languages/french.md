# French: Start Here

**Practise:** [French flashcards and quizzes](/languages/french-practice/) (works on any phone).


I had seven years of French at school and was still terrible at it, so this is my second attempt, this time with comprehensible input, parallel texts and a lot of patience. This page covers how French sounds, the grammar you need first, the essential phrases, and then a numbered path through my French pages.

## 1. Alphabet and pronunciation

French uses the Latin alphabet plus accents, and the accents matter:

| Mark | Example | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| é (aigu) | **été** summer | closed *ay* sound |
| è (grave) | **père** father | open *eh* sound; also separates *à* (to) from *a* (has) |
| ê (circonflexe) | **forêt** forest | often marks a lost *s* (forest → forêt) |
| ç (cédille) | **français** | makes *c* sound like *s* before a, o, u |
| ë / ï (tréma) | **Noël**, **naïf** | pronounce the vowel separately |

Key sounds:

- **Final consonants are usually silent**: *petit* (puh-TEE), *ils parlent* (eel PARL). The exceptions are often C, R, F, L ("CaReFuL"): *sac, mer, chef, sel*.
- **Nasal vowels**: *an/en* (*enfant*), *on* (*bon*), *in/ain* (*vin, pain*), *un* (*brun*).
- **The French *r*** is made at the back of the throat.
- **u** (*tu*) is "ee" with rounded lips; **ou** (*tout*) is "oo".
- **Liaison**: a silent final consonant is pronounced before a vowel: *les amis* (lay-za-MEE).
- Stress falls evenly, with a slight lift on the last syllable of a phrase.

## 2. Core grammar

### Every noun has a gender

*le livre* (m), *la maison* (f). Learn the article with the noun. See [French: Vocabulary](frenchvocabulary.md) for the article cheat sheet and gender clues.

### The three key verbs

| | être (to be) | avoir (to have) | aller (to go) |
|---|---|---|---|
| je / j' | suis | ai | vais |
| tu | es | as | vas |
| il / elle / on | est | a | va |
| nous | sommes | avons | allons |
| vous | êtes | avez | allez |
| ils / elles | sont | ont | vont |

### A regular -er verb: parler (to speak)

| Person | Present | Passé composé | Futur proche |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | parle | j'ai parlé | je vais parler |
| tu | parles | tu as parlé | tu vas parler |
| il / elle | parle | il a parlé | il va parler |
| nous | parlons | nous avons parlé | nous allons parler |
| vous | parlez | vous avez parlé | vous allez parler |
| ils / elles | parlent | ils ont parlé | ils vont parler |

*Je parle, tu parles, il parle* and *ils parlent* all sound the same.

### Negation and questions

- **ne ... pas** around the verb: *Je ne parle pas allemand.* I don't speak German. (In speech the *ne* often disappears: *Je parle pas allemand.*)
- Questions: rising voice (*Tu parles français ?*), *est-ce que* (*Est-ce que tu parles français ?*), or inversion (*Parlez-vous français ?*).
- Note the space before **? ! : ;** in French typography.

### Adjectives agree, and usually follow the noun

*un livre intéressant, une histoire intéressante, des livres intéressants*. A few short common ones come before (BANGS: beauty, age, number, goodness, size): *une belle maison, un petit chat, un bon ami*.

## 3. Essential phrases

| French | English |
|---|---|
| Bonjour ! / Bonsoir ! | Hello / Good evening |
| Salut ! | Hi! / Bye! (informal) |
| Comment allez-vous ? / Ça va ? | How are you? (formal / informal) |
| Ça va bien, merci. Et vous ? | I'm fine, thanks. And you? |
| Je m'appelle VikiD. | My name is VikiD. |
| Enchanté(e). | Pleased to meet you. |
| S'il vous plaît / s'il te plaît | Please (formal / informal) |
| Merci (beaucoup). | Thank you (very much). |
| De rien. | You're welcome. |
| Excusez-moi. / Pardon. | Excuse me. / Sorry. |
| Je ne comprends pas. | I don't understand. |
| Pouvez-vous parler plus lentement ? | Can you speak more slowly? |
| Comment dit-on « ... » en français ? | How do you say "..." in French? |
| Au revoir ! / À bientôt ! | Goodbye! / See you soon! |
