# Spanish: Vocabulary

Two lists: the words I picked up from my own scripts (each one used to be a separate card), and a study-skills word list I built while reading a Spanish article about concept maps and diagrams.

## Words from my scripts

These come from the [Advertencia](advertencia.md) script and my motto.

| Español | Type | English | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| aburrido / aburrida | adj | boring, tedious; bored (with *estar*) | *Lo que decía era tan aburrido que me quedé dormido.* What he was saying was so boring that I fell asleep. |
| acceder (a) | verb | to access, get into; to agree to | *Suscríbete para acceder a más contenidos.* Subscribe to access more content. |
| corriente | adj; nf | ordinary, common; (la corriente) current, electricity | *Es un reloj corriente.* It's an ordinary watch. *Se ha ido la corriente.* The power has gone. **The common currents: the run of the mill!** |
| extrañar | verb | to miss (someone); to surprise | *Cuando viaja, extraña mucho a sus perros.* When she travels, she misses her dogs a lot. *extraño / extraña* = strange |
| házmelo saber | phrase | let me know | *haz* (do/make!, imperative of *hacer*) + *me* (to me) + *lo* (it) + *saber* (to know): "make it known to me". The accent on *há* keeps the stress where it was. |
| malvado / malvada | adj; n | wicked, evil; villain | **Memory trick:** *mal* = bad, so *malvado* = a bad person. (*-ado* isn't really a "person" suffix, but the trick works.) |
| montón (el) | nm | pile, heap; a lot | *Había un montón de fotos antiguas.* There were loads of old photos. |
| tratar (de) | verb | to try (to); to treat | *Trataré de responderte.* I will try to answer you. (*trataré* = future, "I will try") |
