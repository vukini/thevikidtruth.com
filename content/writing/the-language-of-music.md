## The Solution

Well it seems that the first good strategy would be to split out the functions. **Study ‘Music’ separately from your Instrument. Study theory separately from both.** Easy to say. But how do we do it?

### The Third Instrument

We start the discussion with the third, cause I know how impatient the reader is…

I got a good clue for an online course (*that I highly recommend*), called “[Improvise for Real” (IFR)](https://improviseforreal.com/).

To study an instrument, first you must learn to go up and down the instrument chromatically (all 12 notes). One note at a time, then in jumps, like playing **Snakes and Ladders** with it.

You practice going up (and down) in Half-Steps & Whole Notes. It doesn’t sound very musical and we don’t care, it’s almost the point actually.

*Get your brain out of the way and get the body (and ear) familiar with the mechanical actions it needs to perform.*

Most drills taught in music courses actually model this kind of thinking*. The only difference here is that you actively focus on the ‘snake and ladder’ effect and connect the sound with your mind.*

Then (*and this is the Vikid addition*), we practice going up & down in Minor 3rds, Major 3rds, and 5ths. As an extension we can go up in 6th & 7th’s in their varieties etc…

You practice, starting with different fingers and positions, as much as is comfortable or practical on your given instrument.

**Needless to say, use a metronome or equivalent when the basic fingering or whatever, is worked out**.

Once we have done that we start layering the intervals both melodically and harmonically, with scales and chords etc...

At all times we just focus on the mechanics and the sound **without too many labels.** Always smoothly, without jerks at whatever speed permits it, no matter how slow.

*However before doing this, we have to deal with the first two instruments…*

## The First Two Instruments

We all have *two instruments* that we already know reasonably well and more importantly, we know them **fluently**:

1.  Our body

2.  Our voice

**Why aren’t we using them to learn “Music”?**

Without these two, it’s like pretending to go on holiday by reading travel magazines alone or learning a foreign language with *la bouche fermée*.

(A major crime of foreign language instruction)

*That would be highly stupid.*

Yet, that is how I went about learning music for the last couple of decades. Perhaps you are doing that too?

### The First Instrument

Watching my children grow up, the first thing I noticed is that they move their bodies to music from a very young age. No dog nor cat does this, no matter how much music you give it.

**It is our natural language and it is not ‘taught’.**

Much of it comes naturally and from the imitation of others. The basic movements morph naturally to some kind of tapping of the feet, hands or bobbing of the head. We will use this to understand rhythm.

#### What to do

Each morning, for example, put on some *really good music, **music that you love***, and start moving to it.

> **First we get the whole body going.**

I have come the understanding that until we have the big muscles of the body in a groove, the smaller, more intricate muscles used for manipulating your instrument will not get with the program. There will just be **a massive cross-firing of nerve signals** between the large muscles and the small ones and you will continuously be out of time and synchronization.

*More, over you won’t understand why, and think that you just have no talent and no coordination.*

Once the whole body is in the groove for 10 mins or so and with the added benefit of warming you up (feeling awesome too!), **we move to smaller and smaller muscles groups.**

We start tapping to a drum, or for the guitar, we can get into free flowing strum patterns, always ear on the music, mind practically vacant. *I found a sock or corona mask works pretty well for muting the strings.* Your family will thank you!

We are just focusing on ‘feeling’ rhythm. Then, during strums, we move on to picking out singles strings. All the time getting *‘finer and finer’* in movements.

As you get deeper into the music and just feeling the rhythm you can add more and more complexity.

Start singing (your second instrument) while strumming. Let the body get with the grove. Always, body first.

Play air guitar (air piano or whatever!) with your fingers. *Just keep rhythm*. The strings are still muted and you care not what is being played. In your imagination you can be (little) Joe Satriani, Slash or John Mayer.

This form of training is getting your whole body speaking the language of music. No theory, just you, the music and a feeling of freedom.

## The Second Instrument

Most of us know “Do-Re-Mi” from the Sound of Music.

I think this song contains *a hidden gem and that’s why we secretly all love it.*

The Do-Re-Me system is called **Solfege** and it’s beauty is that it is a *relative based system.* **So it doesn’t matter what ‘Key’ you sing in** or how deep / high your voice is, etc... There is a “Fixed Do” system too, but let’s not complicate things.

Solfege, I am guessing also, is probably originally based on the Indian “Sa-Re-Ga-Ma” system and is intuitively how music works in the mind. So that’s what I am thinking we go with.

> **All we have to do is sing and transcribe music using it.**

It will do wonders for **ear training**. It will do wonders for your overall musical abilities, *practical and theoretical.* It will do wonders for your voice.

You need almost nothing. **There is nothing to sell you**, nothing to bottle and that’s probably why the system has fallen out of favor.

You take that stuff and **transcribe** it using Solfege.

> **You then read the solfege daily and singing back the tunes you transcribed.**

## Putting it all together.

You now take it to your instrument. Use solfege to work out the melody on the instrument. **Sing the melody and play at the same time, to build and intuitive connection between mind and instrument.**

Listen again to the song carefully and notice *where the chords change*. The solfege will let you know which chords are being used.

> Slowly but surely you recompose the music on your instrument and you’ve done it in possibly the most musical way it can be done.

**You are speaking the Language of Music with body, heart and mind as a first language. Now, we can go for the grammar and it will actually be useful.**
