**Why Does Nature Keep Producing Squares?** There is a mathematical trick hiding here, but the idea is simpler than the name makes it sound.

If you zoom in closely enough on a smooth curve, you can describe its shape by successively adding simple pieces: a constant, a slope, a bend, then finer corrections.

There is an even deeper reason. Suppose a system is in a stable equilibrium.

Suppose I have some complicated curve describing how the energy of a system changes as I move away from its resting position.

![](/assets/the-demon-who-liked-squares/02ecc429aaa0.png)

**I may not know a simple equation for the whole curve.** But if I only care about what happens **very close to one particular point**, I can build an approximation of it piece by piece.

First I ask:

**How high is the curve at that point?**

That gives me a constant. It’s chosen by us and set to zero.

Then:

**Which way is it sloping?**

That gives me a straight-line correction.

**How much is it bending?**

That gives me a quadratic, or (x²), correction.

Then come progressively finer corrections involving (x³), (x⁴), and so on.
