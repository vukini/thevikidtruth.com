## Where the claim comes from

In September 2024 *The Washington Post*, working with researchers at UC Riverside, published "A bottle of water per email". It said a 100-word email written by GPT-4 needs **519 millilitres** of water, a little more than a standard bottle. The figure spread everywhere, and it still turns up in arguments about AI.

## What's actually true

- **It's a model, not a measurement.** The method comes from the paper *Making AI Less "Thirsty"*. That paper estimated GPT-3 used about 500 ml of water for every **10 to 50** medium-length responses, depending on where and when the model runs. It counts water evaporated to cool the data centre *and* water used to generate the electricity it draws.
- **Most of that water isn't in the data centre.** Critics who went through the method, including writer Andy Masley in correspondence with the paper's lead researcher Shaolei Ren, found that most of the bottle is water used by off-site power plants. That includes evaporation from hydroelectric reservoirs. Ren later told the *Deseret News* that the figure shouldn't be treated as an accurate measure of today's AI. In his words: "We cannot just use a number from two years ago to describe today's system."
- **Newer measurements are far lower.** In August 2025 Google published its own figures: a median Gemini Apps text prompt used **0.26 ml** of water and 0.24 Wh of energy, "about five drops". In June 2025 OpenAI's Sam Altman wrote that an average ChatGPT query uses about **0.000085 gallons**, roughly a fifteenth of a teaspoon (about 0.3 ml). Both are company-reported figures, not independent audits.
- **The total is still real.** Billions of prompts a day add up. Training runs and new data centres also draw heavily on local water and power, especially in hot, dry places like the Gulf.

## The truth

**The truth:** The bottle came from stacking worst-case assumptions about a 2020-era system, then running the result through a headline. Each prompt is closer to a few drops. The real story is the total: how many data centres get built, where, and what power and cooling they use. Ask about that, not about your email.
