# The Standard Model

It is a **simple and comprehensive** theory that attempts to explain all
the hundreds of particles and complex interactions with only:

- 6 quarks
- 6 leptons
- Force carrier particles

All matter particles are composites of quarks and leptons, and they
interact by exchanging force carrier particles only. The theory has many detractors. It doesn't and cant explain
gravity for example.

I use the words **simple** in the sense that **other theories are either more complex or not as
comprehensive**.

[Simple is not easy](simpleisnoteasy.md "MISSING PAGE") however. The mathematics involved here are highly complex.

Below are some of the subjects involved in developing the Standard Model. I shall hope to fill
these pages as I break frontiers.

## The plain idea

The Standard Model is the **periodic table of the fundamental world**: a list of the smallest known building blocks of matter, plus the rules for how they push and pull on each other. Everything you have ever touched, including you, is made of just three of its particles: up quarks, down quarks (bound into protons and neutrons) and electrons.

The full cast:

| Family | Generation 1 | Generation 2 | Generation 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarks (up-type) | up | charm | top |
| Quarks (down-type) | down | strange | bottom |
| Charged leptons | electron | muon | tau |
| Neutrinos | electron neutrino | muon neutrino | tau neutrino |

Then the **force carriers** (bosons): the **photon** (electromagnetism), eight **gluons** (the strong force, which binds quarks), the **W and Z bosons** (the weak force, responsible for radioactive decay and for powering the Sun), and the **Higgs boson**, the visible sign of the Higgs field that gives the W, Z and the matter particles their mass. The Higgs was the last piece found, at CERN's Large Hadron Collider in 2012.

## Why it matters

- **Precision.** It is the most rigorously tested theory in science. Its prediction of the electron's magnetic moment agrees with experiment to roughly one part in a trillion.
- **Predictive power.** It predicted the W and Z bosons (found 1983), the top quark (1995) and the Higgs (2012) before anyone saw them.
- **Technology.** PET scans use antimatter (positrons); radiotherapy, nuclear power and the GPS corrections for relativity all lean on this physics.

Mathematically it is a quantum field theory built on a symmetry [group](groups.md) written \(SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)\): \(SU(3)\) gives the strong force, \(SU(2) \times U(1)\) the unified electroweak force. Each particle is an excitation, a ripple, in its own field filling all of space.

## An everyday analogy: Lego and the rules of snapping

The quarks and leptons are the Lego bricks. The forces are the ways bricks can snap together: some studs snap strongly (the strong force, so strong that quarks are never seen alone), some magnetically (electromagnetism), some only rarely and with a trick (the weak force). The Higgs field is like a crowd at a party: some guests (the top quark) get mobbed and slow down, they are heavy; others (the photon) pass straight through, they are massless.

## A worked example: where does your mass come from?

You might guess your mass is the sum of the masses of your quarks and electrons, which the Higgs field provides. Check it on a proton (two up quarks, one down):

$$ 2 m_u + m_d \approx 2(2.2) + 4.7 \approx 9\ \text{MeV}/c^2 $$

But a proton weighs about \(938\ \text{MeV}/c^2\). The quarks account for **about 1%**. The other 99% is the energy of the gluon field and the frantic motion of quarks inside the proton, turned into mass by \(E = mc^2\). So the Higgs gives quarks and electrons their masses, but almost all of *your* mass is bound-up energy of the strong force. That is one of the most surprising truths in physics.

## What it doesn't explain

As the original text says, the Standard Model has real gaps:

- **Gravity** is not included; combining it with quantum field theory is the great unsolved problem.
- **Dark matter**, about five times as abundant as ordinary matter, is not any Standard Model particle.
- **Dark energy**, driving the accelerating expansion of the universe, is unexplained.
- **Neutrino masses**: the original model had massless neutrinos; oscillation experiments (Nobel Prize 2015) proved they have tiny masses.
- **Matter over antimatter**: the model can't account for why the universe contains so much more matter than antimatter.
- It has around **19 free parameters** (more with neutrino masses) that must be measured rather than predicted.

Physicists have been testing hard for cracks. A long-running anomaly in the muon's magnetic moment ("muon g−2") drew huge attention, but improved theoretical calculations around 2025 brought prediction and the final Fermilab measurement into close agreement, so for now the Standard Model survived that test too.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The Higgs gives everything its mass."** It gives the elementary particles their mass; most of the mass of ordinary matter comes from strong-force energy, as above.
- **"Particles are tiny balls."** They are quantised excitations of fields; "ball" pictures break down fast.
- **"The Standard Model is wrong because it's incomplete."** Within its domain it is spectacularly right. Its successor will have to contain it, as relativity contains Newton.
