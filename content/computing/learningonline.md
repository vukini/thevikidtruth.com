# Learn Online the Vikid Way

Here is the truth about learning computing online: **the net is a library the size of a planet, and without a reading list you will stand in the doorway forever.** The skill is not finding content – it is choosing a thin path and walking it until something clicks.

### What it is, in plain words

Online learning for computing means using tutorials, books, courses, documentation, videos and repositories to build skill – usually alone, usually around a day job. It works astonishingly well if you treat it like apprenticeship (make things, show them, fix them) and fails when you treat it like television (watch, nod, forget).

### Why it matters

Almost everything I know about Linux, Lisp and systems came from man pages, source code, a few great books and building Vikix. University is wonderful for some; it is not the only door. Online learning is how most of the world's working programmers actually learned the tools they use.

### An everyday analogy: a gym versus fitness videos

Watching workouts does not build muscle. Short sessions with a barbell do. Online courses are the video; your editor and terminal are the gym. Prefer pages that make you open a prompt.

### A hands-on method that works

1. **Pick one goal** – "boot Void and tile windows", "write a PicoLisp HTTP handler", "pass a binary to `objdump` and understand it".
2. **Pick one primary text** – an official tutorial, a book, a wiki chapter. Not five.
3. **Work in public or in a repo** – commits beat bookmarks.
4. **Schedule a small stick** – 45 minutes daily beats a heroic weekend monthly.
5. **Teach one thing** – a note on this site, a reply on a forum. Teaching exposes the holes.

### Dealing with overwhelm

When a topic fans out into twelve others, write the twelve down and ignore them. Return only when the current goal needs them. Curiosity is good; unbounded curiosity is a trap that feels like work. A single finished project teaches more than twenty half-finished tutorials.

### Common misconceptions

- **"I need a bootcamp."** Some people thrive there. Many more thrive with a goal, a book and stubbornness.
- **"Free means low quality."** Official docs and wikis are often better than paid courses.
- **"Certificates equal skill."** Projects equal skill. Certificates open some HR doors; they don't compile.
- **"I have to finish the course."** Stop when the goal is met. Courses are means.
- **"Learning alone means alone."** IRC, Discord, mailing lists, local LUGs – ask good questions after trying.
