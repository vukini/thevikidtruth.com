# Learn Pharo with Vikid

I am a beginner at programming SmallTalk & Pharo in particular. The notes/lesson here are my attempt at learning the
language and taking notes on what I have learnt in as clear a way that I can. Hopefully as a beginner you can benefit from
this approach yourself.

- [Object and Messages](objectsandmessagespharo.md)

### What this section is

This is the front page of my beginner's notes on [Pharo](pharo.md) and Smalltalk, written as I learn it myself. Rather than a polished textbook, it is a learner's trail: each linked lesson records what I worked out, in the plainest words I can manage, in the hope that a fellow beginner finds the same path easier.

### Why learn Pharo at all?

Pharo teaches object-oriented programming in its purest, original form, *everything* is an object, and you make things happen by *sending messages* to objects. Because the whole system is live and inspectable (see [Pharo](pharo.md) for what "image-based" means), it is a wonderful place to actually *see* objects behaving, instead of only reading about them. The ideas you pick up here, objects, messages, classes, polymorphism, carry over to nearly every other language you will ever use.

### An everyday analogy

Learning Pharo is like learning to cook in a glass-walled kitchen where nothing is hidden: you can watch every pot, open every drawer, and change the recipe mid-simmer. For a beginner that visibility is gold, you learn by poking at a living system and watching what happens.

### Where to go next

- [Objects and Messages](objectsandmessagespharo.md): the first and most important idea, how everything in Smalltalk is an object receiving a message.
- [Pharo](pharo.md): the bigger picture, image-based programming and the Pharo Zen.

The truth revealed: the fastest way to learn a live system like Pharo is not to study it from outside but to climb in and start sending messages, breaking things, and watching them mend.
