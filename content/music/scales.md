### Major Scales and Modes

There are always two half steps (H) and 5 whole steps (W). All minors have a minor 3rd. All majors have major third.

```
 W W  H W  W W H - Ionian     - Major
 W W  W H  W W H - Lydian     - Major
 W W  H W  W H W - Mixolydian - Major
=====================================
 W H  W W  W H W - Dorian     - Minor
 H W  W W  H W W - Phrygian   - Minor
 W H  W W  H W W - Aeolian    - Minor
```

```
 1   2   3   4  5   6   7   8 - Ionian     - Major
 1   2   3  #4  5   6   7   8 - Lydian     - Major
 1   2   3   4  5   6  b7   8 - Mixolydian - Major
==================================================
 1   2  b3   4  5   6  b7   8 - Dorian     - Minor
 1  b2  b3   4  5  b6  b7   8 - Phrygian   - Minor
 1   2  b3   4  5  b6  b7   8 - Aeolian    - Minor
```

## The plain idea

A **scale** is a ladder of notes, in order, that a melody climbs up and down. Out of the twelve notes available, a scale picks a handful and arranges them around a home note. The **major scale** (Ionian) uses the step pattern W W H W W W H, where W is a whole step (two frets) and H is a half step (one fret). Everything above is a variation on that one ladder.

A **mode** is what you get when you keep the same seven notes but treat a different one as home. Play the white keys from C to C and you get Ionian (major). Play the same white keys from D to D and you get Dorian; from E, Phrygian; from F, Lydian; from G, Mixolydian; from A, Aeolian (natural minor); and from B, **Locrian**, the seventh mode missing from my table:

```
 H W  W H  W W W - Locrian    - Diminished
 1  b2  b3   4 b5  b6  b7   8 - Locrian    - Diminished
```

Locrian's flattened fifth makes its home chord diminished, so it feels unstable and is rarely used as a home key, though metal guitarists love it.

## Why it matters

Scales are the raw material of melody, solos and riffs. Knowing which scale fits a chord progression means you can improvise without hitting wrong notes, and knowing the modes gives you seven different moods from the same notes. Each mode has one or two "colour" notes that define its sound:

| Mode | Colour note | Mood | Heard in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ionian | natural 4 and 7 | bright, resolved | most pop songs |
| Lydian | ♯4 | dreamy, floating | the *Simpsons* theme, many film scores |
| Mixolydian | ♭7 | bluesy, rock | "Sweet Home Alabama", much classic rock |
| Dorian | natural 6 in a minor scale | cool, soulful minor | "Oye Como Va", "So What" |
| Aeolian | ♭6 | sad, natural minor | "Losing My Religion", countless ballads |
| Phrygian | ♭2 | dark, Spanish | flamenco, metal |

## An everyday analogy: the same street, different front doors

Seven houses on a street, the same seven notes. Which house you call *home* changes how the whole street looks. From the C house the street feels bright (major); from the A house the same street feels melancholy (minor). Modes aren't new notes; they're new homes.

## A worked example: hearing Dorian versus Aeolian

Over an A minor chord, play the A natural minor scale (Aeolian): A B C D E F G. Now play A Dorian: A B C D E **F♯** G. One note changes, F to F♯, and the mood shifts from sad to sophisticated. That raised sixth is the sound of Santana, Miles Davis's "So What" and a lot of funk. To find it on guitar, play your A minor pentatonic shape and add the F♯: two frets above the E.

## The pentatonic shortcut

Most beginners improvise best with the **pentatonic** scale, five notes that leave out the two "risky" semitone steps: major pentatonic 1 2 3 5 6, minor pentatonic 1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7. Because there are no semitone clashes, almost every note sounds good over the chords of the key. Add the ♭5 to the minor pentatonic and you have the **blues scale**.

## How to practise (my list, expanded)

- **Walking** – play the scale straight up and down, slowly, with a metronome.
- **Wandering** – improvise freely within the scale, trying to make phrases rather than runs.
- **Scale sequences** – play in patterns: 1-2-3, 2-3-4, 3-4-5…, or in thirds: 1-3, 2-4, 3-5…
- **The value of each note** – hold each note over a drone of the home note and listen to how it feels: stable (1, 3, 5) or wanting to move (2, 4, 6, 7).

## Common misconceptions

- **"Modes are separate scales you must memorise."** They're the major scale started from different notes; learn one, and the rest follow.
- **"Playing a scale is playing music."** Scales are vocabulary. Music is what you say with them.
- **"Minor scale means one scale."** There are three common minors: natural (Aeolian), harmonic (raised 7th) and melodic (raised 6th and 7th going up).
