# phrases
## Greetings and politeness
Habari gani? | What's the news? (how are you?)
Habari za asubuhi? | Good morning (how's the morning?)
Habari za jioni? | Good evening
Nzuri. | Fine, good. | the usual reply to habari
Salama. | Fine, peaceful.
Mambo? | What's up? | reply: poa
Poa. | Cool, fine.
Hamjambo? | How are you all? | reply: hatujambo
Hatujambo. | We are fine.
Karibu sana. | You're very welcome.
Asante sana. | Thank you very much.
Si kitu. | It's nothing; you're welcome.
Pole. | Sorry (sympathy). | for someone's trouble, not an apology
Pole sana. | I'm so sorry for you.

Tuonane kesho. | Let's see each other tomorrow.

Siku njema. | Have a good day.

# sentences
Mimi ni mwanafunzi. | I am a student. | ni = is / am / are
Yeye yuko nyumbani. | He/she is at home. | -ko for location
Chai hii ni tamu. | This tea is sweet. | hii = this (n-class)
Nina kaka wawili. | I have two brothers. | number agrees: wa- for people
Ninapenda chokoleti. | I like chocolate. | ni- I + na- present + penda
Tunaishi karibu na bahari. | We live near the sea. | tu- = we
Leo kuna jua. | It's sunny today. | kuna = there is
Kesho mvua itanyesha. | It will rain tomorrow. | i- agrees with mvua; -ta- = future
Ninaenda pwani Jumapili. | I go to the beach on Sunday.
Basi linaondoka saa mbili. | The bus leaves at eight. | saa mbili = 8 a.m. in Swahili time
Unaweza kunisaidia? | Can you help me? | -ni- = me (object marker)
Siwezi kuja leo usiku. | I can't come tonight. | si- = I (negative)
Ninataka kujifunza Kiswahili. | I want to learn Swahili.
Ninahitaji kufanya kazi kesho. | I need to work tomorrow.
Ninasoma kitabu. | I'm reading a book.
Unafanya nini? | What are you doing?
Dada yangu ni mrefu kuliko mimi. | My sister is taller than me. | kuliko = than
Hiki ni chakula kizuri. | This is good food. | ki- agreement with chakula
Kuna watu wengi barabarani. | There are many people on the road. | -ni = in / on (locative)
Hakuna kitu kwenye friji. | There's nothing in the fridge. | hakuna = there isn't
Sinywi kahawa usiku. | I don't drink coffee at night. | negative present ends in -i
Jana nilikula wali. | Yesterday I ate rice. | -li- = past
Tulienda sinema Jumamosi. | We went to the cinema on Saturday.
Nilipokuwa mtoto, niliishi kijijini. | When I was a child, I lived in the village. | -po- = when
Nimeshatembelea Kenya mara mbili. | I've already visited Kenya twice. | -me- = perfect; -sha- = already
Umekula? | Have you eaten?
Nimeishi hapa kwa miaka mitatu. | I've lived here for three years.
Nilimpa mama yangu. | I gave it to my mother. | -m- = him/her object
Niambie! | Tell me!
Usimwambie. | Don't tell him/her. | u-si- negative subjunctive
Natumaini uko salama. | I hope you're well.
Nataka uje. | I want you to come. | subjunctive ends in -e
Ukifika, nipigie simu. | When you arrive, call me. | -ki- = if / when
Nikipata muda, nitakuja. | If I get time, I'll come.
Kama ningekuwa na pesa, ningesafiri zaidi. | If I had money, I would travel more. | -nge- = conditional
Kichwa kinaniuma. | My head hurts. | literally the head bites me
Kiswahili kinazungumzwa hapa. | Swahili is spoken here. | -w- = passive
Nimefika sasa hivi. | I've just arrived.
Nitajaribu tena. | I'll try again.
Jambo muhimu ni kujaribu. | The important thing is to try.
Je, naweza kufungua dirisha? | May I open the window? | je marks a question
Ingekuwa bora kusubiri. | It would be better to wait.
Tulifurahi sana. | We enjoyed ourselves a lot.
Hatimaye nimemaliza kazi. | I've finally finished the work.
Ninasoma ili nijifunze. | I study so that I learn. | ili + subjunctive
Asante kwa msaada wako. | Thanks for your help.
Sikujua kwamba unasema Kifaransa. | I didn't know that you speak French. | si-ku- = negative past
Kitabu hiki kiliandikwa na rafiki yangu. | This book was written by my friend.
Watoto wanacheza nje. | The children are playing outside. | wa- agrees with watoto
Mti huu ni mrefu sana. | This tree is very tall. | m-/mi- class
Pole pole ndio mwendo. | Slowly is the way to go. | proverb
